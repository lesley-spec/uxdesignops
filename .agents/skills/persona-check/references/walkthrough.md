# Running the walk

The walk is the skill. A list of opinions about a screen is worth nothing; a
step-by-step of one person trying to finish a task finds real breakages.

---

## One step at a time

For each screen or state, four columns and no skipping:

**Looking for** — what they expect, from their mental model, before they see
anything. Written first, or you describe the design instead of the person.

**See** — what is actually on screen. Be literal. Label text, not intent.

**Do** — where they click, or where they stall. "Stalls" is a legitimate answer
and often the most useful one.

**Carry** — the question they take forward unanswered.

**Carry is the column that earns the format.** Abandonment is rarely one bad
screen; it is three screens that each left something unresolved until the person
no longer believes the next one will help.

## Stay in the task

The check fails when it drifts into design critique. Two tells:

- Commenting on something the task never touches
- Using words the persona would not — "hierarchy", "affordance", "IA"

If the task does not take them to a screen, that screen is not in this check.

## Entry state

Set it deliberately. The same screen is a different design depending on arrival:

| | Changes |
|---|---|
| First time vs fourth this week | Whether labels are read at all |
| Mid-task vs starting fresh | How much patience is left |
| Phone vs desktop | What is reachable, what bounces |
| Logged out, mid-flow | Whether the task survives the interruption |
| After a failure | Whether they trust the next screen |

A design that only works from a cold, unhurried desktop start works for almost
nobody.

---

## Ranking

Four levels. Rank by consequence to the person, not by how interesting it is to
us.

| | Means | Goes |
|---|---|---|
| **Stops them** | Cannot finish. Leaves, or does it elsewhere | Into the study, as something to watch for |
| **Costs them** | Finishes, but slower, or by a workaround | Into the study if it recurs |
| **Annoys them** | Finishes fine, feels worse about it | Note it, do not spend a session |
| **Noticed** | A person who looks at interfaces for a living noticed | The list, and no further |

**Most of what a check produces is "noticed."** That is fine and it is why the
ranking exists. An unranked check hands a designer twenty items of equal weight
and they fix the three that are easiest.

## Two things that are never in here

**A number.** No percentages, no "most users". One simulated person is n=0.

**A verdict.** The check does not say whether to ship. It says what to watch for
when real people use it.

## When to stop

When the task is finished or genuinely blocked. A walk that keeps going past the
block is inventing behaviour — the person left.

Record the block as a **stops-them** item and run the task again from a
different entry state instead. Two entry states beat one long walk.
