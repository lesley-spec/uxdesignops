# Synthesis rigor — the four moves

The affinity map is the easy part. These are the moves that decide whether the
output is evidence or a mood board.

---

## 1 — Read raw, not summarised

An AI summary is an interpretation that has already discarded what did not fit
its pattern. Read the transcripts.

**What only appears in raw data:**

| Signal | Looks like | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Tone shift** | Pace changes, a sigh, "honestly…", shorter answers | Marks where the real friction is, often while the words stay polite |
| **Workaround** | A spreadsheet, a second browser tab, a colleague asked | Proof of importance — someone paid a cost to route around it |
| **Say/do contradiction** | "I always check the dashboard" then cannot find it | The stated need and the real one differ; the real one wins |
| **Abandonment** | Task given up, subject changed | Stronger than any complaint |

If you genuinely only have summaries, say so in the card and cap the corpus at
**low** confidence.

## 2 — Separate frequency from importance

Frequency answers *how many brought it up*. Importance answers *what it cost
them*. They are different findings and they often disagree.

```
HIGH frequency, LOW importance   → widely wanted, nobody blocked. Backlog.
LOW frequency, HIGH importance   → narrow and blocking. Usually the real one.
HIGH frequency, HIGH importance  → act. Rare, and obvious when it appears.
LOW frequency, LOW importance    → noise, or a segment you under-sampled.
```

**Evidence importance by behaviour**, not by emphasis:

- built a workaround · abandoned the task · contacted support · lost money or
  time · brought it up unprompted, twice

The last one matters: **unprompted beats prompted.** If the guide asked about
bulk import, "8/8 mentioned bulk import" tells you about the guide.

## 3 — Sweep for disconfirming evidence

For every theme, before you write the card:

- Who in the corpus did **not** hit this?
- Who did the **opposite**?
- What **separates** them from the rest — segment, tenure, volume, role?
- Does anything in the corpus **contradict** the theme outright?

Then write the honest version:

> Most admins (6/8) described bulk import as a priority; the two who did not
> manage fewer than ten users each, where one-by-one is faster than preparing a
> file.

That sentence is more useful than the confident one, and it tells the reader
exactly which users the finding does not cover.

**If you find nothing against a theme, say that you looked.** "No disconfirming
evidence in this corpus" is a finding. A blank field is a gap.

## 4 — Bound the claim to the sample

| You have | You can say | You cannot say |
|---|---|---|
| 8 interviews | "six of eight participants" | "75% of users" |
| One Dovetail project | "in this corpus" | "our partners" |
| One segment | "enterprise admins we spoke to" | "admins" |

And name who is missing. A corpus recruited from people who answered a
recruitment email over-represents people who answer recruitment emails. Say so
in **What this cannot tell you**, every time.
