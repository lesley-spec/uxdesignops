# Guide spec, failure patterns, FAQ

---

## Research panel guide — spec to fill in

```
SUBTITLE
Share your voice, directly influence what we build

TITLE
Join our user research panel

DESCRIPTION
Want a first look at what we're building? Share your feedback in 2–10 minute
studies. We run quick surveys and conversations to inform our product roadmap.
Respond to what matters to you, pause when life gets busy — no long-term
commitment.

PRIMARY BUTTON      Join the panel
SECONDARY BUTTON    Remind me later

TARGETING
├─ Partners who engaged with a feature release guide in the last 90 days
├─ Exclude: has seen this guide before
└─ Estimated segment: 100–200 warm prospects

CONVERSION GOALS
├─ 1  Clicked "Join the panel"
├─ 2  Completed the signup form (external)
└─ 3  Attended the first study

LAUNCH
├─ Duration: 2 weeks
├─ Targets: 15%+ click, 60%+ of clickers complete signup
├─ Success: 8–10 signups, 60%+ of them take part in the first study
└─ Signed off by: ______________        (required — customer-facing)
```

## Failure patterns

### 1 — The cold-audience trap

**Symptom** Shown to everyone, ~1.9% engagement.
**Cause** Temperature, not copy. People will blame the words first; they are
usually wrong.
**Fix** Segment to warm prospects on guide-interaction history. Relaunch to the
smaller audience and compare. Target 15%+.

### 2 — The vague-benefit trap

**Symptom** Impressions are fine, nobody clicks.
**Cause** The copy explains what *you* want, not what they get.
**Fix** Benefit into the subtitle; name the decision in the description. Delete
any sentence starting "we want to understand how you…".

### 3 — The multi-step graveyard

**Symptom** 50% on step one, 2% by step three.
**Cause** Momentum. Each step is another chance to leave.
**Fix** Collapse to one screen; move the form and scheduling to an external
link. A single-step recruitment guide should clear 20% on a warm segment.

### 4 — The no-conversion-goal trap

**Symptom** "10% engagement" and no idea whether the panel exists.
**Cause** Measuring the guide instead of the outcome.
**Fix** All three goals, through to attendance. Until goal 3 is instrumented you
do not have a recruitment number, you have a click number.

## FAQ

**Should I always target warm?**
For recruitment, yes. Cold is defensible for urgent or universal messages —
security, outages, a critical bug. Feature announcements still do better warm.

**How long should a guide run?**
Recruitment: 2–4 weeks, which is roughly how long a warm segment takes to
saturate. Evergreen guides: run on, read weekly.

**5% on a warm audience — what now?**
Copy, most likely. Rotate three variants. If warm targeting is genuinely in
place and copy still will not move it, the offer itself is unattractive — look
at the ask, the time cost, or the incentive.

**Can I recruit cold through Pendo?**
You can, at 1–5%. For cold reach, email a warm list or use a dedicated
recruitment platform (Rally, Respondent). Pendo's advantage is that it catches
people in product, mid-context — spend that on people already paying attention.

**How do I know the panel is real?**
Goal 3. If 60% of signups attend a first study, the recruitment worked. At 20%,
the problem is between signup and session — form length, scheduling friction, or
no follow-up — and no amount of guide copy will fix it.

**Pendo or email?**
Pendo for immediacy and for people who never answer email. Email for anything
needing explanation, attachments, or a considered yes. If the ask needs a
paragraph to make sense, it is not a guide.
