# Copy architecture

The second-biggest lever after targeting. Same audience, different words, 1% to
20%.

---

## The anti-pattern

```
Title:        Join our research panel
Description:  We're building a group of partners who share quick feedback…
```

It leads with the **effort and the ask**. Nothing in it says what the reader
gets. Expect 1–3%.

## The pattern

```
Subtitle:     Shape what we prioritise next
Title:        Join our user research panel
Description:  Want a first look at what we're building? Share your feedback in
              2–10 minute studies. Your input informs our product roadmap.
```

Benefit, then action, then the terms. Expect 15–25% on a warm segment.

---

## Components

| Component | The job | Test it by asking |
|---|---|---|
| **Subtitle** | Benefit, not ask | Would someone read only this line and want in? |
| **Title** | Action clarity | Does the reader know what they will *do*? |
| **Description** | Why me, why now, what's involved | Are the time cost and the exit both stated? |
| **Primary button** | Assumptive, specific | Does it name the thing, or say "learn more"? |
| **Secondary button** | Keeps them warm | Does declining feel temporary? |

**Always state the time cost and be right about it.** "2–10 minutes" is a
commitment. Overrunning it is the fastest way to lose a panel after one study.

**Always state the exit.** "Pause when life gets busy — no long-term commitment"
removes the main unspoken objection to joining a panel.

---

## Button copy

| Weak | Better | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Learn more | Join the panel | Specific action; assumes yes |
| Sign me up | Join the panel | Matches what they actually become |
| Not interested | Remind me later | Keeps a warm prospect in the pipeline |
| Skip | Not now | Temporary decline, not a permanent one |

---

## The three-variant test

Each variant tests a different motivation. Run them to **different segments of
comparable size**, two weeks, one variable changed.

**A — Exclusivity / status**
`"You're invited to shape our roadmap"` — does status-seeking work here?

**B — Community / belonging**
`"Be part of our inner circle"` — does peer validation work here?

**C — Problem-solving / agency**
`"Help us fix what's broken"` — does autonomy work here?

### What to record

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Click rate on the primary button | 15%+ |
| Signup completion, as a share of clickers | 60%+ |
| Participation in the first study | the one that matters |

The winner sets the house voice for recruitment guides until something beats it.
Write the result down somewhere durable — otherwise the next person reruns the
same test.

### Reading a variant test honestly

Three variants across three segments confounds copy with audience. If the
segments differ in temperature, you have measured targeting again. Either hold
the segment constant and rotate copy over time, or accept the result as
directional and say so.
