# Prompt design

Where most research fails: **testing the idea of a thing instead of the thing.**

---

## 1 — Test the thing, not the idea

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ | "Would you use a feature that lets you bulk import users?" |
| ✅ | "Here's the bulk import screen, and here's your CSV. What would you do next?" |

People say yes to anything in the abstract. Agreeing costs nothing and feels
helpful. Put the actual interface in front of them and you watch behaviour
instead of collecting politeness.

## 2 — No hypothetical futures (without a prototype)

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ | "Imagine you could do X in future. Would you use it?" |
| ✅ | "Here's a prototype. Walk me through how you'd use it." |

People are poor predictors of their own future behaviour and generous about
yours. Speculation is not evidence, however confidently it is delivered.

## 3 — Probes specific enough for consistent AI follow-up

In MICRO and SPRINT you cannot human-moderate every session. The AI needs probes
precise enough to ask **the same follow-up of everyone**, or the answers are not
comparable and the sample is really a collection of n=1s.

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ | "Ask them about their workflow." |
| ✅ | "After they describe their workflow, ask: *when you say you use a CSV, do you export from Excel or type it manually?* Then: *how often do you do that?*" |

**Tighter in MICRO** — one pass, no second chance. **Looser in SPRINT**, where
human follow-ups adjust on the fly and can chase what the study missed.

## 4 — No yes/no questions

They are an invitation to avoid admitting failure, and people take it.

### Comprehension

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ | "Do you understand what this feature does?" |
| ✅ | "I'll show you this pricing page for 30 seconds. Without scrolling back — what were the three packages?" |

The second reveals what actually stuck, and nobody has to confess to being
confused.

### Preference

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ | "Which of these two designs do you prefer?" |
| ✅ | "Use the design on the left to do this task. Talk me through each click." then "which would you use for this specific task?" |

Preference is not usability and neither is behaviour. Asked to choose, people
pick one to be agreeable.

### Terminology

| | |
|---|---|
| ❌ | "Is this button label clear?" |
| ✅ | "Read this label. What do you think happens if you tap it?" |

Almost nobody says a label is unclear — that reads as admitting they couldn't
read it. Ask what they expect and the gap shows itself.

## Before it goes out

- [ ] Every question tied to a decision in the hygiene checklist
- [ ] No question answerable with yes or no
- [ ] No hypothetical without a stimulus attached
- [ ] Every probe specific enough to ask identically of everyone
- [ ] Nothing leading — read each aloud and ask which answer it invites
- [ ] A human has reviewed it for domain nuance

Draft all of it. Then a human sets the boundaries and owns what ships.
