Confidence is scored on four things: how many partners or brands were spoken to at that tier, how many independent sources agree, whether behavioural data corroborates the qualitative finding, and how severe the known gaps are. It is a judgement, stated openly so it can be argued with — not a statistical measure.

When two personas conflict: favour Danny among partners, unless the change removes a capability Priya depends on — a simplification is an inconvenience to one and a permanent loss to the other. Favour Maya among brands, because mid-market is both the heaviest user and the group most likely to leave. Favour Nia only on signup, profile and first-link surfaces.

Partner side

For a partner, impact.com is a waypoint. The content, the audience and the earning all happen elsewhere, so every second spent here is overhead on someone else's work. That is why speed is the whole design problem on this side. Grounded in 105 Partner Exp interviews, 119 Mobile App Exp interviews, 12 contextual inquiries and 90 days of behaviour — every percentage carries its real denominator.

Creator and affiliate: where this job diverges
Creator · evidenced

Applications are judged on audience fit, so a rejection reads as a verdict on who follows them. They apply to a handful of brands they would genuinely recommend.

Affiliate · hypothesis

Would apply at volume across categories, filtering on payout and cost model. Silence would be an operational cost rather than a personal one.

What it changes for us: Closing the loop for Nia needs a reason. For an affiliate at the same stage it needs a queue. Same unmet need, two different products.

A day in the life

Nia checks their phone before work. There's a notification: “You're pre-approved, start earning now.” They tap it, land on a list of eligible brands, and apply to four. Six minutes, and it feels like progress.

At lunch they want to add a link to a post in progress. The brand approved last week is there, but the link isn't findable — the page redirects to a desktop view that doesn't fit the screen. They screenshot it to deal with later, and post without the link.

That evening they open the spreadsheet kept since January and write in the two commissions that landed. Of the four applications sent this morning, none will ever be answered. They'll apply again tomorrow.

Creator and affiliate: where this job diverges
Creator · evidenced

“Will this pay back?” is partly a trust question — a bad promotion costs credibility built over years, so they under-promote rather than risk it. Six links per piece of content, by hand, often on a phone.

Affiliate · hypothesis

No audience to protect, so the same question is purely return on effort. Would generate links in bulk and syndicate through feeds or an API; one-at-a-time flows would not scale.

What it changes for us: Expected EPC serves both. But Danny also needs brand-fit signal, and an affiliate needs throughput. Ship the number for both; ship the bulk tooling for one.

A day in the life

Danny films in the morning. By eleven there's a tutorial mentioning six products, and six links are needed before the edit can finish. The laptop comes out and reports open first out of habit — yesterday had plenty of clicks and no conversions, which is more unsettling than a quiet day would be.

Brand by brand. Two are straightforward. One has had its assets moved since the redesign and takes four extra clicks to find. One hasn't been approved yet — applied three weeks ago, heard nothing. That product gets cut from the script rather than waited on.

At four, out with a friend, a follower DMs asking where to buy something from last week's video. The link exists. It cannot be reached from a phone. Danny says they'll send it later, and by evening has forgotten.

Brand side

For a brand, impact.com is a workspace. It is where the programme is built, run, measured and paid — so where partners need speed, brands need depth and control. Almost every brand friction point below is depth or control being withheld.

Three roles, not three company sizes: the ladder is how much of the job is yours — done alongside other work, done as the work, or owned but delegated. Grounded in 37 Gong sales calls, 158 G2 reviews, 43 Brand Exp interviews and PostHog brand cohorts (~13,700 average brand DAU).

Sources. Outset: Partner Exp (105 interviews, 13 studies), Mobile App Exp (119 interviews), Brand Exp (43 interviews, 7 studies), Contextual Inquiry 2026/27 (n=12). G2: 158 brand and advertiser reviews, Jan 2025 – Jul 2026 — 65 mid-market, 58 small business, 21 enterprise. PostHog: 90- and 180-day behavioural pulls taken 8 October 2026.

Reading the numbers. Every percentage states its denominator. Behavioural figures are one-off aggregates — no governed metric definitions exist for them. Device and region counts exclude PublisherPro, which is not browser-attributed. Quotes come from research participants and public G2 reviews. The personas are composites. Names are deliberately gender-neutral and no age, location or pronoun is assigned, because none of those were findings — the behaviour was.

Known gaps. No partner who signed up and never activated has been interviewed. Deal, coupon, review, email and network partners were excluded by the Partner Exp screener, and no partner-type field exists in product data to reconstruct them. No study has recruited outside English, though just under half of partner activity is outside North America. Application submission is effectively uninstrumented, so time-to-decision cannot be measured on either side.
